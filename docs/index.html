San Francisco heat & equity

Surface temperature, fog, vegetation and census demographics across the city’s 41 analysis neighborhoods — and the tools to interrogate all of it yourself.

  1. Heat is governed by fog and canopy. Demographics on their own explain 4% of the variance in surface temperature.
  2. The equity signal runs through canopy: a tract at 90% residents of color runs about 0.4 °C hotter purely by having less of it.
  3. Income has two opposing effects that cancel — it buys canopy, which cools, but wealth also concentrates in the densest built ground, which heats. That is why the raw correlation reads as nothing.
  4. Trees cool most where fog is thickest, not least — the opposite of the obvious prediction.
  5. Therefore tree planting is weakest exactly where vulnerability is highest (r = −0.65 between cooling bought and the vulnerability index).

Method & limits

Surface temperature is not air temperature. Landsat measures the skin temperature of roofs and pavement, far hotter than the air a person stands in. The spatial pattern is meaningful; absolute values are not comparable to a weather station. Landsat also passes late morning, so nothing here describes night-time heat, which is the more health-relevant variable.

The satellite is blind exactly when fog is working. Foggy neighborhoods are seen almost only on their rare clear — and therefore warm — days. Usable observations run from 14 to 41 of 63 scenes, and every neighborhood’s reading is biased warm, by +0.43 to +1.25 °C, with the bias tracking fog at r = +0.58. Every temperature here understates how much cooler the west really is.

Three neighborhoods are parks — Golden Gate Park, McLaren Park and Lincoln Park, 58 to 160 residents. Their census rates are noise, so they are mapped but never scored, ranked or included in any statistic.

Neighborhood median income is an approximation. Medians cannot be summed, so it is a household-weighted mean of tract medians. Every other rate is exact — summed numerator over summed denominator.

The residual spatial clustering was never explained. After fog, canopy, terrain and built-up surface, Moran’s I on the residuals is still +0.433 at p < 0.0001. The spatial error model handles this correctly for inference, but λ = 0.66 is not an explanation of anything — building density, albedo and anthropogenic heat remain untested.

The mediation result depends on one arguable choice. Adding the built-up index to the controls makes every path non-significant. NDBI and NDVI measure opposite sides of the same ground, so conditioning on one while the other is the mediator blocks the path by construction — which is why the headline specification excludes it. Both are shown above.

Mediation on observational data shows a consistent pathway, not a proven cause. And 41 units is coarse: real inequity exists within neighborhoods and is invisible at this resolution.
